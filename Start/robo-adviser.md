## 🛠️ How Robo-Advisors Power Your Accounts

### 1. Automated "Risk-Matched" Portfolios

When you open an ISA or SIPP, you aren't just buying "stocks." You are buying a **portfolio**.

* **The Robo Benefit:** Instead of you researching 2,000 different ETFs, the robo-advisor asks you a series of questions about your goals and "stomach for risk." It then slots your ISA or SIPP into a pre-built "Pot" (e.g., *Level 4 - Cautious* or *Level 10 - Adventurous*).
* **Why it matters for SIPPs:** Since a pension is a 20-40 year game, the robo-advisor ensures you aren't being *too* safe when you're young (missing out on growth) or *too* risky as you approach retirement.

### 2. Automatic Rebalancing (The "Secret Sauce")

Markets move. If your target is 60% Stocks and 40% Bonds, and the stock market booms, you might end up with 80% Stocks. You are now accidentally taking way more risk than you intended.

* **The Robo Benefit:** The algorithm automatically sells the "high" assets and buys the "low" ones to keep your ISA or SIPP perfectly balanced.
* **The DIY Alternative:** In a DIY account (like Vanguard), you have to remember to log in and manually calculate/trade these shifts yourself.

### 3. SIPP Tax Relief Handling

This is the biggest headache for pension beginners.

* **The Robo Benefit:** When you contribute to a SIPP through a robo-advisor, they handle the paperwork with HMRC. If you deposit £800, they automatically claim the **£200 (20%) basic rate tax relief** and drop it into your account usually within 6-11 weeks.
* **Consolidated View:** Most robos show you exactly how much "free money" the government has added to your pot in a clear dashboard.

### 4. Direct Debit & "Rounding Up"

* **The Robo Benefit:** Apps like **Moneybox** or **Plum** can link to your bank account and "round up" your coffee purchases to the nearest pound, sending the spare change into your ISA. It’s a painless way to hit your £20k ISA limit without feeling the "pinch."

## ⚖️ Robo-Advisor vs. DIY (The Trade-off)

| Feature | Robo-Advisor (Nutmeg/Moneyfarm) | DIY Platform (Vanguard/Trading 212) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Effort** | 🟢 **Low** (Set & Forget) | 🔴 **High** (You pick the funds) |
| **Cost (Fees)** | 🟡 **0.45% – 0.75%** | 🟢 **0.15% – 0.25%** |
| **Psychology** | Prevents "Panic Selling" by hiding the noise. | Easy to "fiddle" with and make mistakes. |
| **SIPP Relief** | Fully Automated. | Mostly Automated (on major platforms). |

---

## 💡 Which one should you use for what?

* **Use a Robo-Advisor for your SIPP if:** You have a pension from an old job you want to "consolidate" into one place and don't want to spend your weekends reading financial news.
* **Use a Robo-Advisor for your ISA if:** You are new to investing and want to see how the market "feels" before you try picking your own funds.
* **Avoid Robo-Advisors if:** Your portfolio is over **£100,000**. At this level, a 0.75% fee is £750/year. You could do the same thing on a DIY platform for about £150/year.
