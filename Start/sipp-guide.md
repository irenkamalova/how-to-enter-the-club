# 🚀 Strategy: Maxing Out Your SIPP

The SIPP is essentially a "Tax Time Machine." It lets you take money you would have paid in tax and put it into your own investments instead.

## 1. Understand the "Hard Limits"

Before you dump cash in, you must navigate two main ceilings:

* **The Earnings Limit:** You can only get tax relief on personal contributions up to **100% of your relevant UK earnings** (salary/bonus) in the current tax year.
* *Example:* If you earn £40k, you can only put in £40k gross.


* **The Annual Allowance:** This is **£60,000** for the 2025/26 tax year. This includes your contribution + the government's tax relief + any employer contributions.

---

## 2. The "Carry Forward" Hack (Investing up to £220,000)

If you have a massive lump sum (e.g., £100k) and want to invest it all at once, you can use **Carry Forward**. This allows you to use unused allowances from the **last 3 tax years**.

| Tax Year | Max Allowance |
| --- | --- |
| **2025/26 (Current)** | £60,000 |
| **2024/25** | £60,000 |
| **2023/24** | £60,000 |
| **2022/23** | £40,000 |
| **Potential Total** | **£220,000** |

**The Rules:**

1. You must have had a pension open during those years (even if it had £0 in it).
2. You must use up this year’s £60k first.
3. **Crucial:** Even with Carry Forward, your *personal* contribution still cannot exceed **100% of your earnings this year**. (Unless your employer makes the contribution for you—employer contributions aren't limited by your salary).

---

## 3. The "60% Tax Trap" Rescue

In the UK, there is a "stealth tax" between **£100,000 and £125,140**. For every £2 you earn in this bracket, you lose £1 of your Personal Allowance. This creates an effective tax rate of **60%**.

**The Strategy:**
If you earn £110,000, put **£10,000** into your SIPP.

* Your "Adjusted Income" drops to £100,000.
* You get your full Personal Allowance back.
* You effectively save **60% in tax**. It is the most efficient £10k you will ever spend.


---

## 5. How to Actually Claim the Cash

* **Basic Rate (20%):** Your SIPP provider (Nutmeg/Vanguard/HL) claims this automatically. If you pay in £800, they turn it into £1,000 in your account.
* **Higher/Additional Rate (40% - 45%):** You must claim the extra 20-25% via a **Self-Assessment Tax Return**.
* *Pro Tip:* HMRC usually pays this to you as a cash refund or a tax code adjustment (more monthly take-home pay). **Do not forget to do this**, or you are leaving thousands on the table.



---

### ⚠️ The "Golden Handcuffs"

The catch? You cannot touch SIPP money until age **57** (rising from 55 in 2028). It is a one-way street for a long time.
